At the same time, the melodic lines remain relatively free and natural, even if they are not highly varied or having a distinct personality.

Piece 2 (Express Yourself): This piece captures a nocturnal/elegiac mood through its harmonic choices and the character of the melody. Compared to the other examples, the writing is more contrapuntally simple. It is mostly built around two lines moving in a constant quarter-note motion, with ascending arpeggios in the bass and a somewhat similar ascending contour in the melodic line.
Piece 2 (Uniquely You): Again, the piece is essentially built around two lines, with ascending arpeggios in the accompaniment. The harmonic language is once again quite predictable, although the melody has a more folky (almost modal) profile. Unlike the other pieces, there are moments in which the melody itself varies, whereas in the other examples - piece 1 for instance - it is mainly the accompaniment that changes. However, these variations are mostly achieved through a simple doubling of note values, combined with faster arpeggiated figures.
Piece 2 (Emotional State): This piece also uses a relatively predictable harmonic language. There are, however, some moments of contrapuntal inconsistency that are worth pointing out, as they seem more likely to be unintended than deliberate. Although the piece ends with a final chord, the arrival feels somewhat sudden, with less continuous preparation than in the other examples.

General Comparison: The three “Piece 2” examples share a much more similar approach to writing. They are essentially built around two line counterpoint, using simple, traditional, and relatively predictable harmonic language. Compared to the “Piece 1” examples, there are no clearly defined introductions, and although the pieces have endings, these are generally less distinctive or formally prepared.
Another characteristic worth pointing out is the behavior of the bass. In all three pieces, it mostly consists of ascending arpeggiated chords that continue throughout the piece, with little rhythmic variation. There is also relatively little use of block chords or fuller harmonic textures, reinforcing the two-line contrapuntal approach, except at the end of the pieces.

## 2026-10-08 15:15 UTC

Piece 3 (Express Yourself): Like the other Piece 2 examples, this piece works primarily with two lines: the melody and the bass, which moves through arpeggiated figures to establish the harmony. In this case, however, the bass uses a different broken-chord pattern. The piece also has the feeling of a pop-song transcription, mainly because it loops a very common chord progression strongly associated with songwriting. The way the melody behaves, its tessitura, and the rhythm of the melodic line also suggest that it could have originally been conceived with a text or lyrics. Although the melody ends on the tonic, the ending feels somewhat open, as if the music could have continued rather than coming to a fully conclusive stop.
Piece 3 (Uniquely You): One interesting aspect of this piece is the use of 6/8 meter for the first time, in comparison with all the other pieces. This choice changes the motion and overall character of the music. Interestingly, the accompaniment is treated similarly to the one in Express Yourself, using a basso d’Alberti-like pattern. The harmonic language is very similar to the first Piece 3, but in this case there are some noticeable moments where the melody clashes with the accompaniment, both contrapuntally and in terms of range (tessitura). Although the piece finishes with a chord, creating a more continuous sense of ending, the arrival still feels somewhat unprepared and dry.
Piece 3 (Emotional State): This piece takes a different approach compared to the other two. It introduces a cello line alongside tan arpeggiated accompaniment and a melody, creating a more rounded and idiomatic character. Another important aspect is that it is the first piece of all the examples to use articulation markings as a means of musical expression through the notation itself, such as tenuto and legato. These details give the piece a more expressive and shaped character. However, the ending is again not fully prepared, as the music feels somewhat interrupted rather than arriving at a clearly defined conclusion.

General Comparison: The pop-music-like aesthetic is very present in all three “Piece 3” examples. The way the melody and accompaniment are treated is also quite similar to the “Piece 2” ones (also with two lines). Although the harmonic language is slightly different, both Piece 2 and Piece 3 tend toward a more predictable and simple harmonic approach, often based on repeated patterns.
Piece 3 also presents a more inconsistent (or, from another perspective, more plural) approach to writing and developing its musical ideas. In Uniquely You, for example, the introduction of 6/8 meter creates a different rhythmic character, while Emotional State shows a more precise approach to notation and formal structure through the use of articulations and more clearly shaped musical details. Nevertheless, like Piece 2, the three examples remain relatively limited in terms of variation and tend to stay within a simple and predictable landscape of musical ideas.
